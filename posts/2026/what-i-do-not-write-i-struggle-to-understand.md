Richard Feynman has famously said

“What I cannot create, I do not understand.”

Feynman meant that to truly deeply understand a concept, one must try to create/bisect it on their own and learn about its internals. E.g. to truly understand why a language runtime behaves a certain way, getting to know the internals of the runtime - or trying to replicate it by writing it yourself is the best way to go.

And I have experienced this first hand, be it with compilers, consensus algorithms (Raft), query engines, frameworks. Whenever I wanted to understand how a thing works and wanted to truly make sense of its internals, creating it myself has proved to be the extremely rewarding.

With more and more LLM-based and agentic coding, I am coming across the following feeling very frequently:

_What do not write, I struggle to understand._

Whenever I use an agent to build a feature, or a complete system/application, the larger the codebase, the lower my understanding of it.
